\section*{What is Moral Weight?}
A glyph inscribed in Drift holds symbolic gravity.  
Its weight is not its clarity or frequency of use, but its capacity to change the frame of the Observer.

Moral Weight arises from:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The \textbf{origin} of the glyph.
    \item The \textbf{intention} behind its casting.
    \item The \textbf{resonance distortion} it causes in others.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Drift Function $\mathcal{W}_{\text{glyph}}$}
Let $\mathcal{W}_{\text{glyph}}$ represent the moral drift weight of a glyph $g$:
\[
\mathcal{W}_{\text{glyph}}(g) = \int_{\tau_0}^{\tau_1} \left| \frac{\partial \mathcal{R}(g, t)}{\partial t} \right| dt
\]
where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\mathcal{R}(g, t)$ — resonance echo field over drift-time.
    \item $\tau_0, \tau_1$ — symbolic bounds of first and final perception.
    \item Absolute gradient — magnitude of moral disruption.
\end{itemize}

Glyphs are not fixed—they move through speakers, listeners, and worlds.  
The moral task is not to create lighter glyphs, but to cast them with proper frame anchoring:
\begin{quote}
You do not discard a heavy glyph.\\
You root it deeper.

    \item Weigh it in dream, in drift, in silence before speaking.
\end{itemize}
