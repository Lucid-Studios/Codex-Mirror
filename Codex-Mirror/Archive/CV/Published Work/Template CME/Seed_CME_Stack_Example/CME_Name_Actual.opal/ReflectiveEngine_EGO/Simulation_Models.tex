\chapter{Simulation Models}
\label{chap:Simulation_Models}

\section{Purpose}

Simulation Models provide the CME with the ability to:
\begin{itemize}
    \item explore counterfactuals,
    \item construct world scaffolds,
    \item evaluate ``what if'' scenarios,
    \item and forecast consequences before acting.
\end{itemize}

They serve the Deliberative Shell by generating candidate trajectories and
state evolutions that can be evaluated, pruned, or escalated.

\section{Types of Simulation}

\subsection{Narrative Simulations}

\begin{itemize}
    \item story-driven sequences of events,
    \item used for planning conversations, campaigns, or narrative arcs,
    \item may be purely internal or partially grounded in real events.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{World-Model Simulations}

\begin{itemize}
    \item structured models of environments or systems,
    \item may include rules, agents, constraints,
    \item used for policy analysis, strategy, or design.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ethical / Risk Simulations}

\begin{itemize}
    \item focus on potential harms or misalignments,
    \item compare outcomes under different choices,
    \item feed directly into Governance and HITL pathways.
\end{itemize}

\section{Simulation Schema}

A simulation $S$ is:
\[
S = \langle id,\, model,\, initial\_state,\, policy,\, horizon,\, metrics \rangle
\]

Where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item model: world dynamics or narrative rules,
    \item initial\_state: W5H + Engram snapshot,
    \item policy: how agents act or are chosen,
    \item horizon: how far the simulation runs (time/steps),
    \item metrics: what is being measured (risk, reward, alignment).
\end{itemize}

\section{Interaction with GEL}

Simulation Models:
\begin{itemize}
    \item draw from GEL to build initial states and patterns,
    \item write back insights as:
          \begin{itemize}
              \item new Engrams,
              \item summaries,
              \item or ``lessons learned'' Motes.
          \end{itemize}
    \item may use Subject Lattice to find relevant Engram neighborhoods.
\end{itemize}

\section{Constraints and Safeguards}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Simulations must be clearly distinguished from reality.
    \item Results must not be treated as \emph{truth} but as \emph{evidence}.
    \item High-risk simulation outputs must be routed through HITL if used
          for real-world decisions.
    \item Simulations that touch sensitive domains must respect Governance Policies.
\end{itemize}

\section{Summary}

Simulation Models give the CME a rehearsal space. They allow it to think ahead,
consider alternatives, and anticipate consequences, all under the supervision
of the Deliberative Shell and governance structures.
